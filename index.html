Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Throughout this course, I developed a much deeper understanding of how technology actively shapes the creative process rather than simply supporting it. When working with code in my web-based projects, I realized that structure is not just a background element, but something that directly influences how a user experiences and understands the work. The way pages are linked, how information is organized, and how users navigate through content all contribute to how meaning is created. In my sound and video projects, this became even more clear. Editing software allowed me to control pacing, tone, and emotional impact, showing me that the tools I use are directly involved in shaping the final product. Camera settings, lighting, and resolution also played a major role in how scenes looked and felt, especially in my final project where shooting in 4K allowed for greater control over detail and color. These experiences helped me realize that technology is not separate from the creative process, but is fully integrated into how ideas are formed and communicated. Every technical decision contributes to the overall experience of a project.

2. Embrace experimentation

Across all of my projects in this course, experimentation became a central part of my creative process. In my web-based work, I moved away from traditional linear formats and explored non-linear design, allowing users to interact with content in different ways. This forced me to think beyond standard structures and consider how navigation itself could influence meaning. In my sound and video projects, I experimented with different approaches, including observational footage, essay-based editing, and exploratory storytelling. Each project required a different method, which pushed me to adapt and try new techniques. At times, this process felt uncertain because there was no single correct way to approach the work, but that uncertainty allowed me to take creative risks. Through experimentation, I learned that growth comes from trying new ideas and learning from what works and what does not. This mindset helped me become more flexible and confident when approaching new forms of media, allowing me to develop a more creative and open approach to making digital work.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

The workshop aspect of this course influenced how I approached my projects by encouraging me to think about how my work would be understood by others. During my initial pitch, I had a different concept that focused more on abstract ideas, but I realized that it did not clearly communicate what I wanted. This led me to rethink my approach and develop a more structured narrative for my final project. This process showed me that considering audience perspective is essential to creating effective work. It also helped me become more aware of clarity and communication, ensuring that my projects were not only meaningful to me but also understandable to others. Even without constant feedback, this shift in thinking improved the quality of my work and helped me develop a stronger sense of how to present ideas clearly. Engaging with others in this way made me more thoughtful and intentional in my creative decisions, which ultimately strengthened my projects.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision became one of the most important parts of my process throughout this course. Instead of completing projects in one attempt, I repeatedly returned to my work to make improvements. In my web-based projects, this involved reorganizing layout, refining navigation, and adjusting content to improve clarity and usability. In my sound and video projects, revision meant editing clips, adjusting timing, and improving transitions to create better flow. Through this process, I learned that revision is not just about fixing mistakes, but about strengthening ideas and making them more effective. Each revision improved the overall quality of my work and helped me better communicate my ideas. I also learned that revision looks different depending on the medium, but it is always necessary. This experience helped me develop a more patient and thoughtful approach to creating digital media, understanding that strong work develops over time through continuous refinement.

5. Learn to present your work

Presenting my work became an important part of the creative process throughout this course. Publishing my projects online and sharing my videos on platforms like YouTube made me more aware of how an audience would experience them. I had to consider not only what I wanted to communicate, but also how clearly that message would come across. This influenced how I structured my projects, organized content, and edited my videos. I focused on making my work accessible and easy to understand without needing additional explanation. Presentation also involved thinking about visual clarity, pacing, and overall design. Through this process, I learned that presentation is not just the final step, but a key part of how a project is received. This helped me create work that is both meaningful and engaging for viewers, ensuring that my ideas were communicated effectively.

Final Project

This final project represents the strongest culmination of my work in this course, both technically and creatively. The film follows a woman navigating loss, financial pressure, and isolation, and is told entirely without dialogue. Instead of relying on spoken explanation, I focused on using visual storytelling elements such as lighting, framing, pacing, and transitions to communicate meaning. This required a much more intentional approach to how each scene was constructed and how they connected to one another. I filmed the project using a FujiFilm XT5 in 4K, which allowed me to capture a high level of detail and gave me greater control during the editing process. Shooting in both daytime and nighttime environments, including the cemetery scenes, allowed me to create visual contrast that reflects the emotional progression of the film. I paid close attention to how lighting and color influenced tone, using darker scenes and slower pacing to emphasize isolation, while brighter moments created contrast and variation. Editing was one of the most important parts of this project. I spent a significant amount of time refining how each scene transitioned into the next, making sure the story remained clear even without dialogue. Music and sound also played a key role in guiding the viewer’s interpretation and reinforcing the emotional direction of the film. Overall, this project demonstrates my ability to combine technical skills with visual storytelling, showing growth in both my creative approach and my understanding of how to communicate ideas through digital media.
