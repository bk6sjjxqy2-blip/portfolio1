Composing Digital Media Portfolio

Portfolio Statement

This portfolio brings together a series of coded and experimental digital media projects created through remixing, hacking, and iterative revision. Rather than producing fixed or linear works, these projects emphasize play, instability, and exploration in response to ideas from Derrida and contemporary new media theory. Code is treated as material. Something that is to be reused, altered, and pushed beyond its original purpose.

New Media Webtext Experiment

This webtext experiment was created by hacking and modifying existing code rather than writing from scratch. Inspired by Derrida’s discussion of structure and play, the site resists a single center or linear reading path and instead invites exploration and instability.

Meaning emerges through interaction, navigation, and fragmentation rather than explanation. The web itself functions as the medium, not just a container for content, aligning with the experimental ethos of new media art.

Webtext Experiment: The Cave

The Cave is a web based narrative experiment exploring perception, mediation, and belief through interaction. Meaning is shaped by movement through the site rather than direct explanation.

The project foregrounds instability and play, aligning with Derrida’s concept of decentered structure and new media’s emphasis on interaction.

Reflection

Throughout this course, I learned that composing digital media involves more than technical execution. It requires attention to rhetoric, structure, and audience experience.

This class pushed me to be more creative by encouraging experimentation rather than correctness. Working through these projects helped me understand how code operates on an invisible level shaping interaction and meaning even when it is not immediately seen.

As I became more comfortable modifying and reusing code, I gained a clearer sense of how structure influences creative possibility across media forms.
