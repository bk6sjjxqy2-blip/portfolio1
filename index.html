Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Throughout this course, I developed a much more concrete understanding of how technology actively shapes the creative process rather than simply supporting it. In my web-based projects, working with HTML structure and layout showed me that design decisions—such as how pages are organized and how users move between them—directly affect how meaning is communicated. In my sound and video work, this became even more clear. Editing software was not just a tool to assemble clips, but something that allowed me to control pacing, tone, and emotional impact. In my final project, filming with a FujiFilm XT5 in 4K gave me control over image quality, color, and detail, which I then shaped further through editing. Lighting choices, especially shooting in both daylight and nighttime cemetery scenes, also showed me how technical decisions influence the mood of a scene. Through these experiences, I learned that technology is fully integrated into the creative process.

2. Embrace experimentation

Experimentation became one of the most important parts of my approach to creating media in this course. In my web projects, I explored non-linear design, while in my video work I tested different styles such as observational footage, essay-based editing, and narrative storytelling without dialogue. My final film especially reflects experimentation, as I relied entirely on visuals, lighting, and pacing instead of spoken words. This pushed me to take risks and trust visual storytelling.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

During my initial pitch, I had a completely different idea focused more on abstract emotion through color, but I realized it did not communicate clearly. This made me rethink my project and shift toward a stronger narrative. That early change had a major impact on my final film and helped me focus on clarity and audience understanding.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision became one of the most important parts of my workflow. In my video projects, I constantly re-edited clips, adjusted pacing, and refined transitions. In my final project, I focused heavily on making scenes connect smoothly and ensuring the story flowed clearly without dialogue. Each revision improved the clarity and impact of the work.

5. Learn to present your work

Presenting my work online changed how I approached every project. Uploading videos to YouTube and organizing this portfolio made me think about how an audience would experience my work. I focused on making everything clear, accessible, and visually consistent so that viewers could understand the projects without needing explanation.
