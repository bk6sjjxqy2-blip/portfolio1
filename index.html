Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making
Working with code and web-based projects showed me how much structure and design influence meaning. The way pages are organized, linked, and styled directly shapes how users experience the project. Through building and modifying these sites, I learned that code is not just functional, but also expressive and plays an active role in how ideas are communicated.

2. Embrace experimentation
These projects encouraged me to experiment with non-linear design and interactive elements. Instead of following a traditional format, I explored different ways of presenting information through links, structure, and layout. This helped me move away from rigid formats and think more creatively about how digital media can function.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment
Feedback helped me refine both the design and functionality of my projects. Others pointed out areas where navigation could be clearer or where ideas were not fully developed. This outside perspective helped me improve the overall experience and clarity of my work.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms
I revised my projects multiple times by adjusting layout, rewriting content, and improving navigation. Revision helped me better connect ideas and create a more cohesive experience across pages, showing me that strong work develops over time rather than in a single draft.

5. Learn to present your work
Publishing my work through GitHub Pages made it publicly accessible, which changed how I approached the final product. I had to consider how others would interact with and understand my work, making presentation an important part of the creative process.

Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making
Throughout these projects, I developed a stronger understanding of how digital tools actively shape creative work. While creating the sound composition and podcast, I had to work within the constraints of audio software, which influenced pacing and structure. In the photo essay, editing tools shaped how images were sequenced and how meaning was constructed visually.

2. Embrace experimentation
Each project required me to experiment with different forms of media and approaches. The sound composition pushed me to work creatively with recorded audio, while the podcast explored an open-ended question. The visual projects allowed me to combine text, image, and sound in new ways.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment
Feedback from others played an important role in shaping my projects. It helped me recognize what was effective and what needed improvement, especially in pacing and clarity.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms
Revision was essential in every project. I reworked audio, adjusted visuals, and restructured content multiple times, learning that strong work develops through continuous refinement.

5. Learn to present your work
Sharing my work on platforms like SoundCloud and YouTube made me more aware of audience and presentation, influencing how I finalized each project.
