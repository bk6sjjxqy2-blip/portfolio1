This short film follows a woman dealing with loss, financial pressure, and isolation. The story is told entirely through visuals using lighting, editing, and pacing rather than dialogue. Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Throughout this course, I developed a much deeper understanding of how technology actively shapes the creative process rather than simply supporting it. When working with code in my web-based projects, I realized that structure, layout, and navigation all influence how a viewer experiences and interprets the work. The way pages are linked and organized affects how information is understood, making technology an essential part of meaning-making. In my sound and video projects, technology played an equally important role. Editing software allowed me to control pacing, tone, and clarity, showing that the tools I used directly shaped the final outcome. Camera settings, lighting, and resolution also impacted how scenes looked and felt, especially in my final film where shooting in 4K allowed for greater control over detail and color. These experiences taught me that every technical decision contributes to how a project is experienced, and that understanding technology is necessary to create effective digital media.

2. Embrace experimentation

Across all of my projects, experimentation became a key part of my creative process. In my web-based work, I moved away from traditional linear formats and explored non-linear design, allowing users to interact with content in different ways. This challenged me to think differently about how information is presented and experienced. In my sound and video projects, I experimented with different styles and approaches, including observational footage, essay-based editing, and exploratory storytelling. Each project required a different method, which pushed me to adapt and develop new techniques. At times, experimentation felt uncertain because there was no clear right answer, but this uncertainty allowed me to take creative risks and discover new ways of working. Through this process, I learned that experimentation is not just about trying something new, but about exploring possibilities and learning from the results. This helped me become more confident in my ability to approach new media creatively.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

The workshop aspect of this course influenced how I approached my projects by encouraging me to think about how my work would be understood by others. During my initial pitch, I had a different concept that focused more on abstract ideas, but I realized that it did not clearly communicate what I wanted. This led me to rethink my approach and develop a more structured narrative for my final project. This process showed me that considering audience perspective is essential to creating effective work. It also helped me become more aware of clarity and communication, ensuring that my projects were not only meaningful to me but also understandable to others. Even without constant feedback, this shift in thinking improved the quality of my work and helped me develop a stronger sense of how to present ideas clearly. Engaging with others in this way made me more thoughtful and intentional in my creative decisions.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision became one of the most important parts of my process throughout this course. Instead of completing projects in one attempt, I repeatedly returned to my work to make improvements. In my web-based projects, this involved reorganizing layout, refining navigation, and adjusting content to improve clarity and usability. In my sound and video projects, revision meant editing clips, adjusting timing, and improving transitions to create better flow. Through this process, I learned that revision is not just about fixing mistakes, but about strengthening ideas and making them more effective. Each revision improved the overall quality of my work and helped me better communicate my ideas. I also learned that revision looks different depending on the medium, but it is always necessary. This experience helped me develop a more patient and thoughtful approach to creating digital media, understanding that strong work develops over time.

5. Learn to present your work

Presenting my work became an important part of the creative process throughout this course. Publishing my projects online and sharing my videos on platforms like YouTube made me more aware of how an audience would experience them. I had to consider not only what I wanted to communicate, but also how clearly that message would come across. This influenced how I structured my projects, organized content, and edited my videos. I focused on making my work accessible and easy to understand without needing additional explanation. Presentation also involved thinking about visual clarity, pacing, and overall design. Through this process, I learned that presentation is not just the final step, but a key part of how a project is received. This helped me create work that is both meaningful and engaging for viewers.
