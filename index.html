Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making
Through my work on these projects, I developed a much deeper understanding of how technology actively shapes the process of creation. When working with code and web-based design, I realized that structure is not just a background element but a central part of how meaning is communicated. The way pages are linked, how information is organized, and how users navigate the site all influence how the project is experienced. For example, adjusting layout and navigation changed how clearly ideas were understood and how easily users could interact with the content. This made me realize that technology is not neutral, but instead plays a major role in shaping both form and meaning. Every technical decision, from spacing to linking, contributes to the overall experience. These projects helped me see that digital tools are deeply connected to the creative process and that understanding them is essential to making effective digital media.

2. Embrace experimentation
These projects pushed me to experiment with new forms of digital media rather than relying on traditional structures. Instead of creating straightforward, linear content, I explored non-linear navigation and interactive design. This allowed users to move through the content in different ways, which changed how they engaged with the project. At first, working in this way felt uncertain because there was no single “correct” format to follow. However, through experimentation, I became more comfortable taking creative risks and trying different approaches. This process helped me understand that experimentation is not just about trying something new, but about exploring possibilities and learning from the results. It also showed me that creative growth comes from stepping outside of familiar methods and being open to change. These projects made me more confident in experimenting with structure, design, and presentation.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment
Although my projects were created individually, the workshop environment still influenced how I approached my work. During my initial pitch, I had a different idea that focused more on color and abstract emotion, but it did not clearly communicate my intentions. This made me step back and reconsider how my work would be understood by others. As a result, I shifted toward a more narrative-driven approach that was easier to follow and more effective overall. This experience showed me that engaging with others is not only about receiving feedback, but also about learning to evaluate my own work from an outside perspective. I became more aware of clarity, structure, and audience understanding, which improved the quality of my projects. This shift in thinking helped me create work that is not only meaningful to me but also understandable to others.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms
Revision became a major part of my creative process while working on these projects. Instead of completing a project in one attempt, I went back and made changes to improve structure, clarity, and overall flow. In my web-based work, this included adjusting layouts, reorganizing content, and refining navigation. These revisions were not just small fixes, but important changes that improved how the entire project functioned. I also learned that revision is not just about correcting mistakes, but about refining ideas and making them stronger. Working across different media forms showed me that revision looks different depending on the project, but it is always essential. This process helped me develop a more thoughtful and patient approach to creating digital media.

5. Learn to present your work
Publishing my work online changed how I approached presentation and audience engagement. Knowing that others would be viewing and interacting with my projects made me more aware of how they were structured and presented. I focused on making my work visually clear and easy to navigate so that users could understand it without needing additional explanation. This influenced decisions about layout, organization, and overall design. I learned that presentation is not just about displaying content, but about shaping how it is experienced. This understanding helped me create projects that are not only functional but also engaging and accessible to a wider audience.

Final Project

Video currently private:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rfh4-6SVoA

This short film follows a woman dealing with loss, financial pressure, and isolation. The story is told entirely without dialogue, relying on visuals, lighting, and editing to communicate emotion and progression. I focused heavily on how scenes connect through transitions and pacing, using music and tone to guide the viewer. Shooting in locations like a cemetery during both day and night allowed me to create contrast and reinforce emotional shifts. Filming in 4K with my FujiFilm XT5 gave me control over detail and color, which I refined in editing to match each scene’s mood. This project represents my strongest work because it demonstrates my ability to combine technical skills with visual storytelling.
