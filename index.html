Final Project

This short film follows a woman dealing with loss, financial pressure, and isolation. The story is told entirely through visuals using lighting, editing, and pacing rather than dialogue. Shooting during both day and night allowed me to create contrast and emotional progression, while filming in 4K gave me control over color and detail. This project represents my strongest work and shows my ability to combine technical skills with visual storytelling.

Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Throughout this course, I developed a much more concrete understanding of how technology actively shapes the creative process rather than simply supporting it. In my web-based projects, working with HTML structure and layout showed me that design decisions—such as how pages are organized and how users move between them—directly affect how meaning is communicated. In my sound and video work, this became even more clear. Editing software was not just a tool to assemble clips, but something that allowed me to control pacing, tone, and emotional impact. In my final project, filming with a FujiFilm XT5 in 4K gave me control over image quality, color, and detail, which I then shaped further through editing. Lighting choices, especially shooting in both daylight and nighttime cemetery scenes, also showed me how technical decisions influence the mood of a scene. Through these experiences, I learned that technology is fully integrated into the creative process. Every decision—camera settings, editing cuts, transitions, and layout—contributes to how the audience experiences the work, making technical knowledge essential to creating effective digital media.

2. Embrace experimentation

Experimentation became one of the most important parts of my approach to creating media in this course. In my web projects, I moved away from traditional, linear formats and explored non-linear navigation, allowing users to interact with content in different ways rather than following a fixed structure. This challenged me to think beyond basic organization and consider how interaction itself could create meaning. In my video work, I experimented with multiple styles, including observational footage in my B-roll project, abstract memory-based editing in my essay film, and open-ended questioning in my exploratory vlog. My final film also reflects experimentation, especially in how I used lighting, transitions, and pacing instead of dialogue to communicate the story. At times, this process felt uncertain because there was no clear “correct” approach, but that uncertainty pushed me to take risks and try ideas I would not normally attempt. Through experimentation, I learned that creative growth comes from testing different approaches and adapting based on what works. This helped me become more flexible and confident in working across different types of media.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

The workshop environment influenced my work by forcing me to think about how my projects would be understood by others rather than just how I personally viewed them. During my initial pitch, I had a different concept that focused more on abstract use of color and emotion, but I realized that it did not clearly communicate my ideas. This led me to completely rethink my approach and shift toward a more narrative-driven final project that would be easier for an audience to follow. Even without heavy feedback later on, this early shift had a major impact on the direction of my work. It made me more aware of clarity, pacing, and how scenes connect. I began to approach my projects with the mindset that meaning is not just created, but also interpreted. This helped me make stronger decisions in editing and structure, especially in my final film where I focused on making transitions and progression clear without relying on dialogue. Engaging with the idea of audience interpretation improved my ability to communicate ideas effectively.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision was one of the most important parts of my process throughout this course, especially in my video work. Rather than completing projects in a single attempt, I continuously returned to them to make improvements. In my web projects, this meant reorganizing layout and refining navigation to make the experience more clear and intuitive. In my sound and video projects, revision involved reworking clips, adjusting timing, and improving transitions to create better flow. In my final film, I spent significant time refining how scenes connected, making sure the pacing felt natural and that the story progressed clearly without dialogue. I also adjusted music and visual tone to better match the emotional direction of each scene. Through this process, I learned that revision is not just about fixing mistakes, but about strengthening the overall impact of a project. Each revision made the work more cohesive and intentional. This helped me develop a more patient and detail-oriented approach, understanding that strong media work is built through continuous refinement.

5. Learn to present your work

Presentation became a key part of how I approached my projects, especially when sharing them online. Uploading my videos to YouTube and organizing my work in this portfolio made me more aware of how an audience would experience everything as a whole. I had to consider not only what I was trying to communicate, but also how clearly that message would come across without additional explanation. This influenced how I structured my projects, edited my videos, and organized this portfolio. In my final film, I focused on making the story understandable through visuals alone, using pacing, lighting, and transitions to guide the viewer. In this portfolio, I made sure that each project is clearly presented, easy to access, and visually consistent. Through this process, I learned that presentation is not just the final step, but a critical part of how a project is received. A well-presented project makes ideas clearer, more engaging, and more impactful for the audience.
