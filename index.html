Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Throughout my work in this section, I developed a much deeper understanding of how technology actively shapes the process of making, rather than simply serving as a neutral tool. When working on my code narrative and web-based projects, I realized that the structure of a website—how pages are linked, how content is arranged, and how users navigate—directly impacts how meaning is understood. The way I wrote and modified code influenced not just how the site looked, but how it functioned as an experience. For example, decisions about layout, spacing, and navigation changed how clearly information was communicated and how users interacted with the project. This showed me that technology plays a central role in shaping both form and meaning. Instead of viewing digital tools as something separate from the creative process, I began to see them as integrated into it. This shift in perspective helped me understand that every technical decision, no matter how small, contributes to how a project is experienced and interpreted.

2. Embrace experimentation

These projects pushed me to move beyond traditional formats and explore new ways of presenting ideas through digital media. Instead of relying on a straightforward, linear structure, I experimented with non-linear navigation and interactive design. This allowed users to move through content in different ways, which changed how they engaged with the material. The webtext experiment in particular encouraged me to break away from conventional organization and think about how meaning can be created through structure rather than just content. At first, this approach felt uncertain because there was no single “correct” way to present the project. However, through experimentation, I became more comfortable taking risks and trying new approaches. This process helped me understand that experimentation is not just about trying something different, but about exploring possibilities and discovering what works through practice. It also showed me that creative growth often comes from stepping outside of familiar methods and being willing to adapt.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

The workshop environment played an important role in shaping how I approached my projects, even when the feedback was not always direct or extensive. During my initial pitch, I had a different concept that focused heavily on color and emotion, but it did not communicate clearly or connect as strongly as I expected. This experience made me reconsider my approach and shift toward a more narrative-driven idea. That change reflects how thinking about how others interpret my work influenced my decisions. Instead of focusing only on what I wanted to create, I began to consider how clearly my ideas were being communicated. This shift helped me develop a stronger awareness of audience and interpretation. It also showed me that engaging with others is not just about receiving feedback, but about learning to step back and evaluate my own work more critically. This awareness carried into later projects, where I made more intentional choices about structure and clarity.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision became a key part of my process across all of these projects, and I learned that strong work is developed over time rather than completed in a single attempt. In my web-based projects, I revised layouts, adjusted navigation, and rewrote sections of content to improve clarity and flow. These changes were not just small corrections, but adjustments that improved how the entire project functioned as a whole. I also began to see revision as more than just fixing mistakes; it became a way to refine ideas and strengthen how they are presented. Working across different media forms showed me that revision looks different depending on the medium, but it is always essential. Whether I was editing code, restructuring pages, or refining content, each revision helped create a more cohesive and effective final result. This process helped me develop a more patient and thoughtful approach to creative work.

5. Learn to present your work

Publishing my work online changed how I thought about presentation and audience. Knowing that others could access and interact with my projects made me more aware of how they would be experienced. I had to consider not only what I was trying to communicate, but how clearly that message would come across to someone unfamiliar with the work. This influenced decisions about layout, organization, and overall design. I focused on making my projects more visually clear and easier to navigate so that users could understand them without needing additional explanation. This process showed me that presentation is not just about displaying work, but about shaping how it is received. It also reinforced the idea that design and usability are just as important as content when creating digital media.

Final Project

Video currently private:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rfh4-6SVoA

This short film follows a woman dealing with loss, financial pressure, and isolation. The story is told entirely without dialogue, relying on visuals, lighting, and editing to communicate emotion and progression. I focused heavily on how scenes connect through transitions and pacing, using music and tone to guide the viewer. Shooting in locations like a cemetery during both day and night allowed me to create contrast and reinforce emotional shifts. Filming in 4K with my FujiFilm XT5 gave me control over detail and color, which I refined in editing to match each scene’s mood. This project represents my strongest work in the course because it shows my ability to combine technical skills with storytelling, using visual elements to communicate meaning without explanation.
