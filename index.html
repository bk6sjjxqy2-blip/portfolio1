Course Goals

In the Introduction to New Media projects, I learned that technology is not just a tool but something that directly shapes how meaning is created and understood. Working with code showed me that structure, layout, and navigation all influence how a user experiences a project. The way pages are linked and organized changes how information is interpreted, which made me more aware of how technical decisions impact communication. I also explored experimentation by moving away from traditional formats and using non-linear design, allowing users to move through content in different ways. This pushed me to think more creatively and take risks in how I presented ideas. Revision became an important part of my process, as I adjusted layout and structure multiple times to improve clarity and usability. Finally, presenting my work online made me think more about audience experience, ensuring that my projects were clear, organized, and easy to navigate.

Course Goals

In the Sound and Image section, I learned how different forms of media interact to create meaning and how technology shapes that process. Working with audio showed me that sound editing is not just about recording, but about controlling tone, pacing, and clarity. Adjusting volume levels, layering sounds, and organizing clips all influenced how the audience experienced the project. I also experimented with combining sound, visuals, and text, exploring how these elements work together rather than separately. This required me to think beyond traditional formats and consider how different media forms could be integrated into one cohesive piece. Revision was a major part of improving my work, as I adjusted timing and structure multiple times to improve flow and clarity. Presenting these projects also helped me understand how audiences interpret sound and visuals together, which influenced how I finalized each piece.

Course Goals

Through my Moving Image projects, I developed a stronger understanding of visual storytelling and how technology shapes meaning in film. Filming showed me that camera angles, framing, and movement all affect how a viewer interprets a scene, while editing allowed me to control pacing and emotional impact. I experimented with different video styles, including observational footage and essay-based editing, which helped me understand how flexible video can be as a medium. Revision became one of the most important parts of the process, as I reworked clips multiple times to improve clarity and flow. Editing was not just a final step, but a core part of shaping the project. Presenting these videos on platforms like YouTube made me more aware of audience experience and pushed me to make my work more clear and engaging.

Final Project

Video private:

This short film follows a woman dealing with loss, financial pressure, and isolation. The story is told entirely through visuals using lighting, editing, and pacing rather than dialogue. I focused on how scenes connect through transitions and tone. Shooting in different lighting conditions created contrast, and filming in 4K allowed detailed control over image quality. This project represents my strongest work because it combines technical skill with visual storytelling.
